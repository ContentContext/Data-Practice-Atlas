Function

The Atlas is an archive for people who work with data or are interested in working with data. It gathers the books worth arguing with, the people and studios worth following, the festivals worth visiting and the portals, odd datasets and tools worth an afternoon.

An item is listed if it is useful as a tool, relevant to independent practice, and easy to reach. Every entry ends with access: where to find it, and how sure we are of the link.

Data governance and constraints

Provenance. A personal list, expanded with Claude on 19 Nov 2026 in several rounds, plus a Tool category that reproduces Andy Kirk's Visualising Data resources catalogue (293 tools, read the same day) in its seven original sections, without descriptions.

Corrections. On the Visualising Data catalogue page about a dozen links sit on the wrong tile (for example Vega and Vega-Lite, Plotly and Dash); the Atlas corrects them and says so on each entry. RightsCon 2026 (Lusaka) was cancelled. "Super dot" is Superdot (Basel); its Venice work is listed as the Come ViVi Venezia project.

How the pictures are made. Treemap groups use each entry's first theme. The decade chart only counts entries with a year. Network positions are computed: each theme sits at the average position of the categories its entries belong to, and each entry sits between its category and its themes. Theme families are an editorial grouping: every theme belongs to exactly one family, and each entry's family is stored in the data (tools are always Tools; otherwise the family with the most themes, the first theme counting a little more, regions only when nothing else applies); each family has one shape, one pattern and one ink, used the same way in the network, on the entry plates and theme lines, and in the footer wordmark. On catalogue entries, the theme-family ink also colours the top rule block and the category tab; categories keep their own inks in the charts, network and table.

Gaps. Leans towards Europe, North America and English-language work despite deliberate additions from Latin America, Africa, MENA, South and Southeast Asia and Italy. Years and places are blank where not confirmed. Event dates change.

Permitted use. A starting reading, contacts and tools list for teaching, inspiration and programme research. Not for citation without checking each link, claims of completeness, or ranking people or studios.
